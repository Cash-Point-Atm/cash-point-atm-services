Why This Number Matters

Smaller withdrawals signal immediate spending intent.

Customers withdraw significantly smaller amounts at in-store ATMs compared to bank-attached ATMs. The average is $68. This isn't coincidence — it reflects behavior. They need cash right now, for something right here.

When someone pulls $68 from your ATM, they are not depositing it into savings. They are spending it in the next few minutes, in the next few feet. Your store is the nearest place to spend it. This is the mechanism behind the 20% higher spend rate that appears consistently across independent studies.

Source: CDS Consumer Habits in Retail ATMs
What the Data Means

Understanding why
these numbers hold.

The statistics above appear consistently across multiple independent research organizations over many years. They're not outliers. They reflect predictable human behavior at the point of purchase.

Understanding the mechanism is just as important as the number itself. Cash proximity drives spending. Convenience drives loyalty. Foot traffic that enters for one reason stays for another.

01

Proximity drives purchase

When a customer withdraws cash from your ATM, they're physically inside your business with money in their hand. The conversion rate from "used the ATM" to "bought something" is over 50%. That's not marketing — that's proximity.

02

Convenience creates loyalty

When customers know your location has an ATM, it factors into their decisions about where to go. 35% explicitly say they'd visit less without it. The ATM becomes a reason to choose you — not just a machine in the corner.

03

Cash behavior is different from card behavior

Multiple behavioral economics studies show that people spend cash differently than they spend with cards. Cash feels more tangible, which paradoxically makes people more willing to spend it on small, immediate purchases. Your average-ticket items benefit directly.

04

The effect compounds over time

A customer who comes in once for the ATM and buys something is a good transaction. A customer who establishes a habit of using your ATM regularly — and buying something each visit — is a different category entirely. Retention matters more than acquisition.
